# Sources for the exogenous concept layer

The opportunity space cannot be derived from the brands being measured. If it is,
every position has a claimant by construction and unoccupied ground can never
appear. These are the external sources used to establish positions that were
available to the category whether or not anyone took them.

## Patient burden (X01–X10)

- Bell RA, Kravitz RL, Wilkes MS. *Direct-to-consumer prescription drug advertising, 1989–1998: a content analysis of conditions, targets, inducements, and appeals.* J Fam Pract. 2000. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10840918/
- Applequist J, Ball JG. *An Updated Analysis of Direct-to-Consumer Television Advertisements for Prescription Drugs.* Ann Fam Med. 2018;16(3):211. https://www.annfammed.org/content/16/3/211
- *The Atopic Dermatitis Patient Journey: From Awareness to Advanced Care.* https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12949480/
- Buhl T, et al. *Atopic dermatitis — perspectives and unmet medical needs.* JDDG. 2023. https://consensus.app/papers/details/b1f0d7d0958859809fe56d87dadecada/

Sleep disturbance is consistently reported as among the most burdensome symptoms
in moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis; anxiety and depression comorbidity is
well established; caregiver sleep loss in paediatric disease is documented.

## Clinical barriers (H01–H10)

- Gooderham M, et al. *Canadian Consensus Guidelines for the Management of Atopic Dermatitis with Topical Therapies.* Dermatol Ther. 2025. https://consensus.app/papers/details/62650e33ba3e5308949b0ad3d2889114/
  - Establishes maintenance non-corticosteroid therapy after clearance to prevent
    flares and reduce TCS need (→ H04, H05), and shared decision-making with
    patient-reported outcomes alongside physician-rated measures (→ H07).
- Papadimitropoulos E, et al. *Burden, Treatment Patterns and Real-World Barriers to Prescribing Advanced Treatments in Adults with Atopic Dermatitis.* Dermatol Ther. 2025. https://consensus.app/papers/details/c1c3294ed714593580366ed905a969c8/
  - Cost, coverage and formulary restriction as the dominant prescribing barriers (→ H08).
- *Topical corticosteroid phobia in atopic dermatitis: nature, origins and frequency.* Br J Dermatol. 2011. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21671892/
- *Corticosteroid Phobia in Patients with Atopic Dermatitis: A Narrative Review.* Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol. https://www.dovepress.com/corticosteroid-phobia-in-patients-with-atopic-dermatitis-a-narrative-r-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID
  - Corticophobia undermines adherence and worsens outcomes (→ H01, H03).
- *New Topicals in Atopic Dermatitis: Evidence, Barriers, and Real-World Perspectives.* HCPLive. https://www.hcplive.com/view/new-topicals-atopic-dermatitis-evidence-barriers-real-world-perspectives
  - Social-media-driven topical steroid withdrawal narratives actively undermining
    first-line adherence (→ H02).
- *Perceived adherence and associated barriers to the national atopic dermatitis guideline: a survey among general practitioners.* https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10443994/
- *Corticophobia and adherence to topical corticosteroids in atopic dermatitis treatment in southern Brazil.* https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11074558/
  - Dermatologists most comfortable prescribing TCS; paediatricians most hesitant;
    GPs report lowest guideline adherence (→ H06).
- *Tolerability of and Adherence to Topical Treatments in Atopic Dermatitis: A Narrative Review.* https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8019006/
  - Application-site tolerability as an adherence driver (→ H09).

## Upgrade path

Congress transcripts (AAD, EADV, Fall Clinical, SPD) are the better source for
H-series concepts than published literature, because they capture what clinicians
say to each other rather than what survives peer review. Symposium Q&A in
particular surfaces the objections brands are actually failing to answer. Not used
in this prototype — published literature stands in as a proxy.
