01

The regulatory content comes out first.

Safety information, indication statements, prescribing links, disclaimers — nobody chose a word of it, and its volume varies by molecule rather than by marketing. What the label determines is set aside too. Only what somebody decided gets scored.

02

Every territory is named, including the empty ones.

The list is not built only from what these brands say — an inventory drawn from the sites has a user for every territory by construction. Half of it comes from the published literature on what patients and clinicians say is missing.

03

Every number is one click from its evidence.

Nothing appears that cannot be traced to the wording on a brand's website, or the image, that produced it. Click a figure and the quote opens on the page it came from, highlighted. That is the tool's entire claim to credibility.

What it will not do

A number you cannot check is worth nothing.

This reads public websites in one market on one date. It is a snapshot of what a category publishes, not a verdict on its strategy — and the report says so, in the same detail it says everything else.

Not the whole message

Websites only. Congress activity, sales aids, field and MSL messaging, paid media and social are outside it.

Not a regulatory read

Availability ratings are a strategist's assessment. Anything worth pursuing still needs medical, legal and regulatory review.

Not hidden

Every rule applied is published on the result page, applied identically to every brand, and open to challenge.
